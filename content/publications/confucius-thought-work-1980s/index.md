abstract: "The Chinese Communist Party actively promotes Confucius today, yet until the 1970s it criticized him harshly for his emphasis on social hierarchy. Why did a party that calls itself a class party come to propagate a figure it had long attacked? This paper explores why Confucius was re-evaluated in political thought work during the 1980s, combining qualitative and quantitative content analysis of Party organs with archival material on the Confucius Culture Festival held in Qufu, Shandong. The bonds that made re-evaluation possible were already in place in the early 1980s, and propaganda followed in the middle of the decade. The shift is read not as a mere policy change but as a change in how the Party understood the effectiveness of its own ideological legitimacy — a corrective to accounts that treat Tiananmen as the decisive turning point."
