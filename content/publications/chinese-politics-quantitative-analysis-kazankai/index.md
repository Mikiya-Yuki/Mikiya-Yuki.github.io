abstract: "This commissioned essay describes research on the Chinese Communist Party's propaganda policy: what information the Party chooses to publicize or withhold, and how it presents that information. It explains how quantitative text analysis became a way to make content analysis more reproducible and, with access to Chinese archives limited, one strategy for contributing to the field alongside archival and qualitative work. It also reflects on balancing interpretability against methodological validity, and on how to weigh new findings that diverge from earlier scholarship."
